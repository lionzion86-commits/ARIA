Your rights, in plain language

Florida first, in plain words. Upload your policy and Aria will tell you how each of these applies to you.

You can dispute a decision.

If you disagree with a denial or with the amount, you can tell your insurer in writing and send your evidence: photos, estimates, receipts.

You can ask for it in writing.

Ask the insurer to explain the denial or the payment in writing, including the policy language it relied on. You can also ask for a complete copy of your policy.

You can get a public adjuster.

A licensed public adjuster works for you, not for the insurer. You can check a license with the Florida Department of Financial Services before you sign anything.

You can ask about mediation.

The Florida Department of Financial Services runs a mediation program for many residential property claims. Ask whether yours qualifies.

You may have more options.

Many policies have an appraisal clause for disagreements about the amount, and a claim can sometimes be reopened or supplemented when new damage appears.

Deadlines matter.

Reporting a claim, supplementing it and filing a lawsuit all have time limits, and Florida has changed some of them in recent years. Your policy states yours: upload it and Aria will find them.

Is it really free?

Yes. Aria Home Shield is free for homeowners. If you hire a professional, their fees are between you and them, and they should explain them before you sign anything.

Who is Aria?

Aria is your expert guide, on your side. She knows claims, policies and repairs the way a lawyer, a public adjuster, a contractor and an agent do. She looks at your situation and your policy first, so you know exactly where you stand, and then connects you with the right licensed pro, through Aria Home Shield.

Will my phone start ringing?

No. Nobody gets your contact until you approve a specific pro, and only that pro can reach you.

What happens to my policy when I upload it?

It is stored privately and used to review it for you. A pro sees it only if you connect with them. You can delete it at any time from your case page.

Do I need damage to start?

No. Many people start with a policy review, before hurricane season, to find out whether their coverage is weak.
